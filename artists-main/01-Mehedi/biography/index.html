Professional Biography

Mehedi Islam Rafi is a Bangladeshi contemporary visual artist and doctoral researcher at the Faculty of Fine Art, University of Dhaka. He holds both a Bachelor of Fine Arts (BFA) and a Master of Fine Arts (MFA), and is currently pursuing his PhD in Fine Arts.

Bangladeshi visual artist with participation in international and national exhibitions, art fairs, collaborative projects, workshops and art camps across Asia, Europe and North America. His exhibition record includes presentations in Mexico, Nepal, Malaysia, Sri Lanka, Indonesia, Greece, Thailand, Japan, the United States and Bangladesh.

Working primarily in watercolour and acrylic, his practice is shaped by the rivers, boats, rural settlements, agricultural landscapes and working communities of Bangladesh. Through fluid washes, atmospheric colour and economical brushwork, he transforms observed moments into poetic visual narratives.

“The river is not only a landscape; it is a living archive of labour, movement, memory and belonging.”

Artist Statement

My paintings grow from close observation of the landscapes and everyday life of Bangladesh. Rivers, boats, villages, fields, markets and ordinary travellers become visual records of a culture continually shaped by water, labour and movement.

Watercolour is central to my practice because its transparency and unpredictability mirror the changing atmosphere of the river landscape. I allow washes to flow, merge and leave areas of open paper, using light as an active element rather than merely an effect. In acrylic works, I extend this interest through layered surfaces, broader tonal fields and more sustained colour.

The river appears repeatedly as both subject and metaphor. It carries livelihoods, memory, migration and the continuity of community. By painting fishermen, ferry crossings, village architecture and agricultural life, I seek to honour people whose daily experiences are often overlooked while also recording landscapes that are changing rapidly.

My aim is not to reproduce a scene literally, but to preserve its emotional atmosphere. I want the viewer to sense wind, humidity, distance, silence and human presence. Through these works, I hope to create a visual dialogue about cultural heritage, ecological awareness and the dignity of ordinary life.

PRACTICE SUMMARY

Mehedi Islam Rafi's practice combines studio-based observation with direct engagement in collaborative art camps and international exchange. These environments support his continuing research into landscape, river-based communities, cultural memory and the expressive possibilities of watercolour.
