About the artist

Rivers, cities and memory, rebuilt in light

Born1998, Shariatpur
Lives & worksDhaka, Bangladesh
NationalityBangladeshi
PracticeInstallation, sculpture, new media

Sharif Hawlader turns the landscapes and crafts of Bangladesh into immersive spaces of light, projection and sound.

Born in 1998 in Shariatpur, where the Padma eats away its own banks every monsoon, he builds work around loss, memory and change. River erosion, rising water and the Sundarbans mangroves become rooms a visitor walks through: projected shorelines collapsing beside real earth, rain rendered as falling strands of light, a forest that breathes on curved screens.

He brings the same approach to Bengal's material culture. Nakshi kantha stitching, jamdani weaving and rickshaw painting are re-made through generative and algorithmic processes, while works such as Invisible Labour turn to the garment workers whose hands keep the country's economy running. Bangla letterforms themselves become suspended sculpture in Language in Light.

Trained through sculpture, he moves between physical material and digital form. Since his first solo exhibition in 2018 at the National Sculpture Gallery, he has held seven solo shows and exhibited across South, Southeast and Central Asia and Türkiye.
